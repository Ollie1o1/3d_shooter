# Chapter 3: beyond the road

Brainstorm, October 31, 2026. No Chapter 3 map, boss, portal or gravity system
has been implemented. The direction is a gradual transition from the physical
industrial world into an otherworldly existence with increasingly impossible
layouts. Hell, another dimension and an uncertain boundary between them are
all still open.

## Recommended premise

**The machines keep doing recognizable work, but the space they work on has
stopped obeying reality.** Chapter 1 sorts bodies. Chapter 2 clears roads.
Chapter 3 starts routing places themselves.

Keep the service road as the opening connection. The player walks toward the
tower, enters a transit structure and gradually discovers that the route is
physically impossible. Familiar materials and jobs make the first violations
more unsettling. A clear objective still anchors each encounter: restore a
crossing, release a lock, reach a visible doorway.

## Three tonal routes

| Route | What the player experiences | Narrative potential |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Industrial hell | Roads over a furnace horizon; condemned machinery endlessly repairing its own ruins; production continuing beneath the world | Work becomes punishment. The creator may have built a system even its operators cannot leave. |
| Failed dimension | Freight halls folded into themselves; ash hanging motionless; a sea overhead; different versions of the same factory | Infrastructure crosses realities. UNIT 24 may be a rejected version rather than a defective machine. |
| Uncertain crossing | Evidence for both: industrial records insist this is a normal district, while geometry and inhabitants contradict them | Let the player feel the transition before receiving an explanation. Preserve room for a later reveal. |

The uncertain crossing is the strongest starting point: commit to the
experience and visual language while keeping the cosmology open.

## Possible chapter names

- **THE WRONG SIDE** — clear sense of having crossed something irreversible.
- **THE INTERCHANGE** — an ordinary infrastructure name that acquires another meaning.
- **THE BELOW** — works for hell or a place beneath physical reality.
- **THE UNMEASURED** — connects impossible space to machines trying to understand it.

## A possible six-beat route

1. **The Return Road.** A quiet approach. Road markings repeat; the tower
   appears closer through a side opening than through the front entrance. The
   first anomaly can be observed without a fight.
2. **The Transfer Hall.** A transit gate takes the player to the other side of
   the same room, but the sky is now below its windows. Establish one predictable
   rule for crossing the boundary, with a safe chance to repeat it.
3. **The Hanging Works.** Fragments of a factory hang over a dark sea. Broad
   ledges, grapple rails and visible destinations support a combat encounter
   across impossible-looking architecture. Reuse familiar enemy behaviour here
   so the environment can be the new thing.
4. **The Waiting District.** A quiet street of service booths, each containing
   the same stopped clock. One machine asks the player to hold its place in a
   queue. A short record describes a concrete spatial fault rather than
   explaining the universe.
5. **The Fold.** Returning power rearranges a junction. A previously distant
   platform now borders the player's room; a former exit is visible high on a
   wall. Players use the established rule intentionally, opening an optional
   route before the main fight.
6. **The Surveyor.** A machine built to measure routes tries to correct the
   player as a spatial error. It marks a strip before removing/replacing it,
   unfolds bridges and exposes its measuring head after committing. A parried
   measurement briefly pins the layout in place. The machinery and collision
   must agree on every change.

## Geometry ideas worth prototyping

- A long staircase brings the player back above its own entrance.
- An outdoor opening reveals a familiar factory room standing vertically.
- An entire street is reflected overhead; the reflection moves one beat late.
- A doorway leads to a larger exterior than the building could contain.
- Two visible versions of a room have different missing floors; switching
  between them creates a route neither version supplies alone.
- A huge machine crosses the horizon, but its footsteps come from below.

These are a menu of possibilities. A full chapter needs one coherent spatial
rule and a few memorable violations, developed through traversal and combat.

## First playable experiment

Build a five-minute sequence: ordinary road → first impossible reveal → one
repeatable spatial transition → a small encounter using that transition → a
view of the larger surreal world. Use existing weapons and enemies first.

Start with authored geometry, moving structures and controlled transitions.
Seamless portals and freely rotating gravity are separate engineering projects;
prototype either only if this sequence shows that its gameplay depends on it.
Ordinary movement can already carry much stranger architecture than the first
two chapters. Keep attack tells, aim and landings dependable as space becomes
unreliable, and reserve an accessible route through required traversal.

## Questions for the next design conversation

- Should the crossing feel like descent into punishment, accidental discovery,
  or entry into infrastructure the machines were never meant to see?
- Is UNIT 24 uniquely able to pass through it, or have other units gone ahead?
- Should the first resident be hostile, helpful, or occupied with a mundane job?
- Which single spatial interaction should players learn and then master?
