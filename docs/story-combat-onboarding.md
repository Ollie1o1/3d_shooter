# Teaching combat through Story

Design proposal. These encounters are ideas for the next gameplay pass; the
text cleanup described below is implemented separately.

## Encounter rule

Introduce one decision in a low-pressure space, repeat it with one complication,
then combine it with an established skill. Let players discover an advantage
without requiring one exact move to progress. Use cover, silhouettes, sound,
projectile timing and reachable platforms to demonstrate the opportunity.

Show a short, binding-aware action prompt when the player can act. Dismiss it
after success. Keep explanations in the optional Field Guide. A death or retry
should not replay a speech or a sequence of tutorial cards.

## Suggested progression

| Style | First encounter | Follow-up | What success should feel like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deliberate revolver shots | After UNIT 01, one exposed enemy with a readable approach and room to aim. | A ranged enemy firing across a lane, with a solid machine to break sightlines. | Stop firing blindly, use cover, land a controlled shot. |
| Aggressive recovery | An isolated, wounded finisher target in an early factory fight. Show the interaction key only within reach. | Put the next wounded target on the far side of low cover while one slow enemy approaches. | Moving in restores health and reloads the weapon. |
| Parry and punish | A lone shooter behind a protected approach, firing slow, separated projectiles. | Pair the shooter with a slow melee enemy, keeping a clear escape lane. Later, introduce a single white melee tell. | Hear the cue, return the shot, then take advantage of the opening. |
| Shotgun mobility | Near the shotgun pickup, offer a low optional ledge and a normal ramp to the same destination. | An elevated firing angle overlooking two enemies, with a clear landing area. | Recoil creates a useful route and a better attack angle. |
| Grapple aggression | At the grapple pickup, teach a fixed anchor before placing a light gunner on a reachable side deck. | A light gunner and a heavy enemy offer different grapple outcomes in a familiar room. | Pull a light enemy in or move toward a heavy one deliberately. |
| Environmental control | The Dead Span's existing winch overlooks enemies and a clearly marked impact zone. | Combine the winch with a ranged enemy that encourages a different approach to the switch. | A deliberate action changes both the enemy count and the available cover. |
| Interrupt and reposition | In Motor Assessment, one suspended enemy fires across the apparatus while both floor routes remain open. | Add a flanker only after the player has had a chance to interrupt the beam or reach cover. | Switch targets, break the attack, then relocate through the machinery. |
| SPLINTER setups | After its pickup, place one visible weak point at an awkward angle, with an ordinary shooting route available. | Two targets on different elevations reward setting up SPLINTER and changing weapons. | A prepared shot solves an angle that direct fire makes awkward. |
| RECALL planning | Near the module, provide a safe overlook and a short, visible danger lane. | Return to a familiar shape of encounter with a vent cycle or a flanking enemy. | Choose a safe return point before committing, then use the statue's lure or shatter intentionally. |

## Chapter identities

1. **Cradle:** aim, close-range recovery, parrying, shotgun movement, then grapple.
   Revisit earlier skills before the Sorter. Keep the first few minutes focused
   on the salvage discovery and one understandable fight at a time.
2. **Dead Span:** longer sightlines, elevation and machinery. The loading gantry
   should offer a useful firing angle while its lower route provides cover.
3. **Living Ward:** interrupt dangerous enemies and change routes. Make the
   assessment circuits useful against beams and committed lunges. Keep the
   Conservatory quiet so the next encounter has room to land.
4. **Blood Moon Plain:** use the skills together across a moving environment.
   Introduce each new weak point visibly before adding pressure. Teach SPLINTER
   on a modest target before expecting it to help against the Gleaner's brood.
5. **Cold Store:** plan a route, commit, then recover. Vents and RECALL should
   create decisions about timing and safe positions. Let the Frozen Sea remain
   a visual break between combat sections.

## First implementation slice

Start with the Cradle's first three fights: controlled revolver shots, a finisher
opportunity, and a protected projectile-parry opportunity. Observe whether a
new player notices the opportunity, attempts it and repeats it voluntarily.
Only add the next complication after that behavior is understandable.

## Implemented text cleanup

- Boss introductions show the name alone. The Gleaner's reveal has no purpose
  caption or spoken catchphrase, and its ending has no redundant status caption.
- Automatic boss taunts, delayed speeches and death quotes are removed,
  including the Successor, Sorter, Ballast, Absolute, 24-K and Restorer.
- Repeated Sorter attack narration is removed; its visual and audio tells and
  the floor-collapse warning remain.
- Gleaner objectives and unfamiliar mechanic notices use short, direct actions.
- Optional records and information that explains the story remain available.
- This pass does not introduce the proposed encounters or change boss balance.
